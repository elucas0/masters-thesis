On-board processing promises to overcome the downlink latency and bandwidth constraints that limit the usefulness of Earth observation (EO) data for time-critical applications such as flood and wildfire response.
Geospatial Foundation Models (GeoFMs) are attractive candidates for this role because their large-scale, multimodal pre-training yields semantic feature extractors that generalize across tasks with few labeled examples.
However, GeoFMs are pre-trained on archived data from missions such as Sentinel-2, and their behavior when exposed to a new sensor is unknown.
This thesis investigates whether TerraMind, a Vision Transformer (ViT)-based GeoFM, produces domain-invariant latent representations when applied to real imagery from the European Space Agency's $\Phi$-sat-2 CubeSat mission, whether a lightweight CNN distilled from it can be made sensor-invariant, and whether doing so is preferable to simply training the compact model on the target sensor directly.

Using a co-registered triplet dataset of real $\Phi$-sat-2, real Sentinel-2, and physics-simulated $\Phi$-sat-2 imagery, we show that the domain gap is present from the encoder's early layers.
This domain gap is measured using embeddings distances of two sensor views of the same ground in TerraMind's embedding space.
Furthermore, a linear probe transfers only a third of its source-domain accuracy to the target sensor.
This finding is consistent with a low-level, radiometric origin (differing spectral response functions) rather than a semantic one.
Naively fine-tuning on physics-degraded data does not close this gap.
Domain-specific batch normalization, applied to the student during cross-architecture knowledge distillation into a lightweight CNN overcomes this gap as it inverts every measurement above (cosine similarity $0.098 \rightarrow 0.948$, probe transfer $33\% \rightarrow 97\%$) while requiring only that the sensor of each input be known, not that inputs be paired across sensors.
A paired-contrastive objective exploiting the co-registered pairing directly was also implemented and evaluated, but added no measurable benefit on top of domain-specific normalization alone.
Whether this adaptation is actually worth adopting is answered conditionally: the distilled, domain-adapted student outperforms a supervised baseline of identical architecture trained directly on the target sensor at a representative labeled-data budget (mIoU $0.4532$ vs.
\ $0.3989$), but loses to that same baseline at a much smaller one (mIoU $0.2679$ vs.\ $0.2916$), reversing significantly in both directions.
A planned few-shot comparison against $\Phi$satNet, an on-orbit GeoFM developed for the same mission, remains to be run.
% The results position deliberate, domain-aware adaptation -- not incidental exposure to degraded data, and not necessarily the more complex paired mechanism -- as a necessary condition for realizing the generalization promise of GeoFMs on board resource-constrained EO missions, while showing that the value of doing so is itself label-budget-dependent rather than unconditional.
